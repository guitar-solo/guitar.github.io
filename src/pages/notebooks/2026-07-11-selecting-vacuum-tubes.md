One of the pedals that's remained on my pedalboard since the late-1990s has been the Ibanez Tube King TK999 MIJ. A true 
bypass switching system (preserving signal tone) and a rugged metal construction (designed for years of reliable use) 
are the features that attracted me to the pedal. What's kept me around this long are the tonal versatility and 
consistently full-bodied sounds produced by the circuitry.

The "Made in Japan" (MIJ) version was manufactured between 1994 and 1995 before Ibanez moved production to the United States.
The Japanese version has an onboard noise reduction system lacking in the American production runs. The noise reduction 
circuit has a switchable on/off control and a variable threshold when enabled. While effective at lower thresholds, the 
reduction system can heavily affect playability at higher settings. 'Just enough' is the magic strategy.

The interior casing has a tendency to rust. I've owned two of these pedals over my lifetime and both have succumbed to 
internal surface rust. You'll see in the photos that I've needed to resurface the baseplate and internal-facing 
surfaces. Rust-Oleum sells a fast-drying spray coating (Bright Coat) that's formulated to prevent rust and provides a 
glossy, durable, and metallic finish. It wonderfully freshens up the pedal.

The TK999 circuitry operates as a tube-driven preamp stage, featuring a dual-triode vacuum tube socket with an elevated 
plate voltage. The original factory tube is a Sovtak 12AX7. Historically, I've replaced this tube with a JJ 12AX7 
because the overall output volume from the original was always insufficient, requiring much higher than expected master 
volume setting to achieve a unity gain through the entire signal chain.

As I've aged, I'm settled into using Mesa Boogie 12AT7 preamp tubes. These tubes are manufactured by Tung Sol and 
provide lower preampfification gain in exchange for increased headroom, harmonically-rich saturation, and a smoother 
breakup that just feels natural. Each string maintains its own fundamental characteristics and definition. Plucks are 
fluid, dynamic, and responsive to the touch. Sensitivity is king. I've heard some folks describe this as a 
"musical response" from the tube.

| Usage  | Type           | Brand       | Characteristics              |
|--------|----------------|-------------|------------------------------|
| Preamp | 12AX7 / ECC83S | Tung-Sol    | Amplification Factor: 100    |
| Preamp | 12AT7 / ECC81  | Mesa-Boogie | Amplification Factor: 60     |
| Preamp | 12AU7 / ECC82  | JJ          | Amplification Factor: 20     |
| Preamp | 12AX7A / ECC83 | Bugera      | Reduced Noise / Microphonics |
| Power  | EL84 / 6BQ5    | Bugera      | Amplification Factor: 100    |

The TK999 also features an active 3-band EQ: Bass, Mid, and Treble.

* **Bass**: low-frequency boost (100 Hz, 0 - 28 dB boost)
* **Mid**: midrange emphasis (1 kHz, 0 - 25 dB boost)
* **Treble**: high-frequency clarity (3 kHz, 0 - 22 dB boost)

This is not my favorite part of the pedal. In fact, I'm actively looking at supplemental equalization pedals to better 
shape the overall spectrum of audio.

As a requirement for any unique pedal, there are some oddities. The input and output jacks on the top of the chassis 
are reversed, making it somewhat awkward to wire up cleanly. The overall size of the pedal is quite large too. And, the 
control knobs are easily knocked off of their shaft at the most inconvenient of times. But I can work around these 
quirks for a refreshing level of saturation and sparklingly tone.
